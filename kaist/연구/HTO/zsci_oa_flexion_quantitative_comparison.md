## TL;DR

**Knee flexion ROM during stance is substantially reduced in OA patients** compared to healthy controls, with the clearest quantitative evidence showing roughly 7.7° less motion. Loading-response excursion reductions are consistently reported but fewer studies supply discrete degree values.

----

## Knee flexion ROM during stance

Several studies directly compare stance-phase knee flexion between OA patients and healthy controls. The most explicit quantitative values come from a sensor-based case-control study using the gaitWALK system.

| Group | Knee Flexion ROM – Stance (°) | Knee Flexion ROM – Swing (°) |
|---|---|---|
| Knee OA patients (n = 23) | 10.3 ± 4.0 | 54.8 ± 5.5 |
| Healthy controls (n = 21) | 18.0 ± 4.0 | 61.2 ± 6.1 |
| Reduction due to OA | **~7.7°** (p < 0.001) | ~6.4° (p = 0.003) |

These values represent total sagittal-plane excursion across the entire stance phase, not solely the loading-response peak [1]. The magnitude of stance-phase reduction (7.7°) is statistically significant and was used alongside swing-phase ROM to discriminate OA patients from controls with a sensitivity of 0.952 and specificity of 0.783 [1].

----

## Loading response and early stance values

The loading-response (first flexion peak after heel strike) is consistently noted as reduced in knee OA, though fewer papers report precise degree values for both groups in this sub-phase specifically.

- **Flexion angle at foot contact** was significantly lower in patients with moderate and severe OA than in healthy subjects, and flexion excursion during loading response was also reduced, with these differences scaling with disease severity [2].
- **Knee flexion excursion during loading response** in an OA cohort showed significant positive correlation with the peak knee flexion moment and significant negative correlation with the knee adduction moment angular impulse, indicating that reduced loading-response excursion shifts mechanical stress to the frontal plane [3]. 무릎을 위아래/앞뒤로 부드럽게 굽혀서 충격을 덜어내지 못하니까, 그 튕겨 나오는 힘이 무릎을 안쪽/밖쪽으로 꺾으려는 좌우 회전력(**Knee adduction moment angular impulse**) 형태로 바뀌어 무릎 내측 관절에 고스란히 물리적 압박으로 전달
- **A coordination study** confirmed reduced knee flexion during the stance phase in KOA, with the authors explicitly noting that decreased knee flexion during the loading response may reduce vertical kinematic synergy and contribute to disease progression [4].
- **A Brazilian dissertation** (Arantes, 2006) reported significantly lower knee ROM specifically during the loading-response phase (p = 0.019) and across all of stance (p = 0.046) in elderly women with knee OA compared to asymptomatic controls, but did not publish discrete mean degree values in the available abstract [5].

No paper in this corpus provides the exact heel-strike angle paired with the first flexion peak for both OA and control groups in a single table, limiting calculation of discrete excursion values for loading response alone.

----

## Patellofemoral OA early stance exception

One study on patellofemoral joint OA (PFJOA) reports a directionally opposite finding compared to tibiofemoral OA, warranting separate consideration.

| Group | Peak Knee Flexion – First Half of Stance (°) |
|---|---|
| PFJOA with pain flare (n = 12) | 17.7 ± 2.4 |
| PFJOA without pain flare (n = 10) | Not reported separately |
| Healthy controls (n = 10) | 13.6 ± 3.4 |

In this PFJOA cohort, the pain-flare group had **higher** peak knee flexion during the first half of stance than controls (p = .003), alongside a higher knee flexion moment impulse [6]. This is mechanistically distinct from tibiofemoral OA, where the cardinal finding is a reduction in early-stance knee flexion; the PFJOA result reflects increased sagittal-plane loading rather than quadriceps avoidance.

----

## Supporting evidence from surgical intervention studies

Pre-operative data from HTO cohorts — who have medial compartment tibiofemoral OA — independently confirm that reduced stance-phase knee flexion is a consistent OA-related finding, and that its magnitude is clinically meaningful.

- **Lind et al. (2013)** found that maximum knee flexion in stance was significantly reduced pre-operatively in medial OA patients compared to controls (p = 0.02), and that this normalized after HTO (p = 0.005), though exact degree values were not reported in the abstract [7].
- **Whatling et al. (2020)** similarly demonstrated smaller sagittal-plane knee ROM during gait in pre-HTO OA patients vs. controls (p = 0.003), and this deficit persisted post-operatively, suggesting it is not fully corrected by realignment alone [8].
- These surgical-control comparisons corroborate the stance-phase ROM reduction (~7.7°) found in the direct OA vs. control study [1], reinforcing that this quantum of sagittal deficit is a robust feature of knee OA gait.
